How a broke shop attendant in Ibadan turned his phone into a PDF-selling machine

No laptop. No design skills. No money to lose.

You know that moment past midnight when the shop has finally locked up, your legs still ache from a full day on your feet, and your phone is the only light left on in the room? You type something into Google you'd never say out loud to anyone — something like "how to make money in Nigeria with just a phone."

You open tab after tab. Every single one starts the same way: "First, download this software..." You don't have a laptop. You close it. The next one wants ₦20,000 "to get started." You don't have ₦20,000 — you barely have transport money for the week.

Why does every solution assume you already have what you don't have?

Meanwhile, the salary you already know by heart finishes before the month does. Rent. Transport. That one contribution you can't say no to. By the 25th, you're counting change in your head while you're supposed to be attending to customers — smiling, ringing up sales, your mind somewhere else entirely.

You've tried a few things already. A WhatsApp "masterclass" that turned out to be someone's old screenshots. A Telegram group promising weekly payouts that vanished with people's money. A YouTube tutorial that said "just open Canva on your laptop" — you closed it before he finished the sentence.

You're not lazy. You're not slow. You just haven't been shown a method that starts from where you actually are — a phone, a data plan, and not a single naira to risk.

I'm going to show you exactly how Tobi, a shop attendant in Ibadan, created his first digital product — and made his first sales — using nothing but his phone and free AI tools, in under 24 hours.

This didn't start with him. It started with me failing.

Before I ever ran this blog, I was quietly failing at every "make money online" idea you can name. I tried affiliate marketing. I tried reselling phone accessories on Instagram. I paid ₦15,000 for an "AI course" from a page that stopped replying the day after I sent the money.

The part I never told anyone: I didn't own a laptop either. Every tutorial assumed I did — "open your dashboard," "install this software," "connect your card." I had a phone, a prepaid data bundle, and a growing pile of quiet shame.

For close to two years, I convinced myself that online money simply wasn't built for someone like me. Maybe it was for people with laptops and foreign cards, not someone stretching data to load one page at a time.

But something wouldn't let me stop looking.

One night, instead of searching "how to make money online" again, I typed something more honest: "what can I actually sell using only my phone?" That small change in the question changed everything.

The same answer kept showing up: digital products. Specifically, PDFs. Short guides. Cheat sheets. Templates. Things people were already paying for on Selar and WhatsApp every single day — things that needed no shipping, no shop, and no laptop to create.

I still hit a wall. What would I even write about? How do I design a cover without a designer? How do I price it? Who would even buy from a total stranger?

So I did the only thing left to do. I stopped buying courses and started testing.

I spent weeks running every free AI tool I could reach on my phone — testing prompts until I found the ones that produced a real, structured PDF instead of the generic one-paragraph nonsense most people get. I tested cover-design apps until I found the ones that actually worked on a low-end Android without crashing halfway through. I underpriced a product and barely sold any. I overpriced another and sold none. I tested captions, posting times, Selar versus bank transfer, until a pattern started to hold.

Slowly, a repeatable system came together — boring, unglamorous, and built entirely around the idea that the only tool you own is your phone.

Then Tobi messaged me

Twenty-seven years old, working the counter at a phone accessories shop in Ibadan, saving toward finally moving out of his parents' house — and getting nowhere. He'd tried a few of the same dead-end guides I once had, for the same reasons I had.

I sent him the raw notes of my system. Not a polished course. Just the steps, in order.
